How we work

A manager you can check on the state record

DN239MANAGEMENT LLC is a Florida limited liability company with document number L23000525593 on public record with the Florida Division of Corporations, formed in 2023. The work is rental property management in southwest Florida: tenant placement, rent collection, deposit handling, maintenance coordination and owner reporting.

Two Florida rules matter more than anything else an owner could ask about. First, managing or leasing real property for another for compensation is a licensed activity under Chapter 475 of the Florida Statutes, requiring a broker's license or a sales associate acting under a broker. Managing your own property needs no license; doing it for someone else for a fee does. Ask any manager for the number and check it against the Department of Business and Professional Regulation's public record, ours included. Second, under Statute 83.49 a security deposit must sit in a separate account for the tenant's benefit or be bonded, must never be commingled with operating funds, and the tenant must receive written notice within thirty days of how it is held. We also do not manage condominium or homeowners associations, which needs a separate CAM license we do not hold.

Frequently asked

The questions we get most

Does a property manager really need a license in Florida?

To manage or lease property for someone else for compensation, yes — that is regulated real estate activity under Chapter 475 requiring a broker's license or a sales associate under a broker. Managing your own property needs nothing. The gap matters because plenty of people manage for owners without a license, and an owner relying on them is relying on someone who cannot lawfully do it. Ask for the number and verify it with the state.

Where is my tenant's security deposit actually held?

In a separate account for the tenant's benefit, or secured by a surety bond — never mixed with operating money. Florida Statute 83.49 requires that, and it requires the tenant to receive written notice within thirty days saying how it is held, where, and at what interest rate if any. If a manager cannot tell you which account a deposit is in, that is the answer to your question.

Can I keep the deposit for the damage they left?

Only by following the statutory procedure and its notice periods. A claim against a deposit that is not made the way the statute requires is forfeited, which is how owners who were plainly owed money end up with none of it. We handle the move-out notices properly rather than deducting and hoping.

Can you manage our HOA as well?

No. Community association management above the statutory thresholds requires a separate community association manager license under Chapter 468, which we do not hold and do not claim. It is a genuinely different regulated activity rather than an extension of rental management, and we will refer you to a licensed CAM.

I would rather not rent to a particular kind of tenant. Can you screen for that?

No, and an instruction of that sort ends the conversation. Tenant selection follows the Fair Housing Act and applicable state and local law, with the same written criteria applied to every applicant. Consistency is what makes a refusal defensible; making exceptions is what generates complaints.

What rent can I get, and will you guarantee occupancy?

We will give you an honest view of what the market will carry and we will not guarantee income, occupancy or yield. If the rent you want is above the market we will say so, even though a higher rent would mean a higher fee for us — because a property sitting empty for two months to protect a headline number costs you more than the difference.
